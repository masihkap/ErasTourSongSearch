Taylor Swift Eras Tour Setlists: Documentation

The information I chose to represent for my visualization was what songs Taylor Swift played during the Eras tour and whether it was part of the regular setlist or a surprise song.



I found the skeleton of the data through a Reddit post in the Taylor Swift subReddit. This data had information in multiple tabs regarding what stadiums she played at, what songs were part of the setlist and what surprise songs were played at each stop. I took this data and created three separate CSV files - one with the stadiums and dates she played there (TaylorSwift_ErasTour_DatesStadiums_Geo.csv), one with the regular setlists (TaylorSwift_ErasTour_RegularSetlist.csv), and another with the surprise songs (TaylorSwift_ErasTour_SurpriseSongs.csv).

The data required editing for discrepancies, additional information I wanted to add, and to tie the three sheets together. In the DatesStadiums_Geo file I added a Show_ID primary key to create uniqueness, whether the show was canceled or not (Vienna, Austria was canceled due to security concerns), a Setlist_ID column, and a Latitude and Longitude column. While the setlist did not change for every show, it did change a couple of times and some of those songs ended up as surprise songs later on - such as Long Live - so I wanted to properly showcase that in my visualization. I knew I wanted to note where the song was played using a map to display the information and in order to utilize GeoJSON, I needed the latitudal and longitudal information. For full disclosure, I did use ChatGPT to assist with getting the coordinates for the stadiums.

The original data source I used only tracked two setlist changes - prior to the release of The Tortured Poets Department album and post release when a new section was added to the set and folklore/evermore were combined into one. I knew there were a couple other changes, including some with special guests based on if they were opening or available, which I wanted to properly track. In the RegularSetlist file, I added each setlist and tied the songs to a set using the Setlist_ID column that I created. The Setlist_ID and Song created a composite primary key. I used Setlist.fm to obtain the setlist changes and surprise songs. The Setlist_ID column joined to the Setlist_ID column in DatesStadiums_Geo to track which show used which setlist.

The original data source had most of the information I wanted for the SurpriseSongs file. Information I added was whether it was a mashup, if it was the first or second song played, and if there was a surprise guest. The mashups in the file were mostly displayed as SongTitle x Songtitle but a couple were SongTitle/SongTitle. I cleansed the data and put all songs on their own row to avoid messing with parsing song titles in my code which would also speed up data returns. I also added the Show_ID column to tie to the DatesStadiums_Geo file to see which show had which songs.

Once I had the data how I wanted it, which in hindsight was probably overkill but my data engineer brain had to fix it, I was ready to work on my visualization. I did not draw any physical documentation because I had what I wanted in my head. I wanted a textbox that the song table could be entered in that would return some information about how many times that song was played on tour, whether it was part of the regular setlist, whether it was a surprise song, and if it was played as a surprise song was it played on the guitar (song 1) or piano (song 2). For the visualization component, I wanted to show on a world map where the song was played.

The basic setup was fairly straightforward. The color palette I chose were remniscent of Eras colors while also being considerate toward colorblindness. The indigo for Speak Now, midnight blue for Midnights, gold for Fearless, medium purple for 1989, hot pink for Lover. I steered away from reds (for Red) and greens (for folklore) because I was worried about colorblindness. I stayed away from orange (reputation), brown (evermore) and grey (the Tortured Poets Department) because they were either too boring, too bright, or too light. I took a screenshot of my final visualization and uploaded it on Colblindor to make sure things looked okay. Later on when it came to displaying where a song was played on the map, I chose to use hearts and stars instead of dots for my marks. This is another Taylor Swift reference - she started making heart hands during the original Fearless album tour. A lot of songs have star references and when you order items from her store, little star (or heart) tissue paper cut outs are included. I also thought this would be a good distinction if there were color blindness issues to still get the mapped data across, even if the colors ended up being similar. In the instances that a song was played in a location, I made the star symbol a little larger than the heart to both further highlight it but if there was a heart overlaid, to try and peak out from the heart. The black outline for each symbol was to tame the color brightness and make them easier to see as I feel yellow kind of "bleeds" into white and the edges get lost.

The map was an obstacle I did not imagine having. I knew I had to add in latitude and longitude but then finding a worldview map instead of a US map that I could get the visualization to work with was difficult. The initial map I found was from an http website. It worked fine utilizing VS Code's Live Server extension but Github does not allow use of http - it must be https. I tried changing that map to https but received a "Mixed Content" error that I had to hunt down in the console. It was difficult to find a world map that just had light country outlines. I do not remember how I found it; there were a lot of google searches and attempts. While I had other challenging parts to the visualization, this was probably the most anxiety inducing because it worked in VS Code without issue but in Git Pages, nothing worked without the map.

The other issue I faced was trying to get the songs to count correctly - especially songs in the regular setlist. Initially it would just count the setlist instances - in this case, it would say a normal setlist song, like "Karma", was played 8 times. I needed to write a loop that would check the setlist_ID in the RegularSetlist file then count the instances that song was in a setlist for each location in DatesStadiums_Geo. Some songs, like "Long Live", "tis the damn season", "invisible string" and "the 1" were included in the regular setlist at certain points but then later came back as surprise songs. For these, I needed to make sure the original count mechanism picked them up for only those specific tour dates and then added instances from the SurpriseSongs file. When showing the surprise guest, I had to do a similar check on both the RegularSetlist and SurpriseSongs file and make sure either instance was returned.

The bar charts were very last minute additions. I started writing this documentation page, feared my visualization was too boring and added it in. Getting the data was the easy part; figuring out how I wanted it to look and scaling it properly was the most difficult part. I'm not entirely sold on having it but I felt if someone who wasn't familiar with her music still wanted to see what the visualization did, it would give them some song titles to enter. Since they remained static, placing them before the input and map seemed to be the best spot. There are a few tweaks I would have liked to do given more time and more skill. I would have liked to add a list of locations the song was played at. There were also a few songs, like "Karma" and "no body, no crime" which had a few shows that included a special guest but not all of the shows. Ice Spice was only a special guest on "Karma" during the three East Rutherford, New Jersey songs despite Karma being the ending song of every show on the tour. "no body, no crime" was part of the regular setlist for eight shows when HAIM was one of the openers but was played once as a surprise song without them. "florida!!" was played four times but all four times included special guest Florence Welch. I would have liked to add in these specific details but at this point, I'm scared to break the visualization before turning it in.
